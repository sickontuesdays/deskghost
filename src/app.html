DeskGhost

A Destiny 2 Ghost shell that floats around your Windows desktop. Made by Stephen Litzinger, a member of the S!ck on Tuesdays! clan, for the Destiny 2 community. Free, non-commercial, and not connected to Bungie in any way.

How it works

  • It draws its own window. The Ghost lives in a transparent, click-through window that Windows places on top of your desktop. Mouse clicks pass straight through it to whatever is underneath.
  • It never touches other programs or games. It does not load code into them, read their memory, hook them, or send them input. It only asks Windows about window and monitor positions, reads your mouse position, and shows or hides its own window.
  • Shells come from Bungie's public content servers. On first run it downloads Bungie's public list of Ghost shells and shaders; each shell's model and textures download the first time you pick it. All of it is cached on your PC, so it works offline afterwards.
  • No account, no sign-in, no API key. It cannot see your Destiny account, characters or inventory, and it has no way to change anything in the game.
  • Nothing is collected or uploaded. No telemetry, no analytics, no accounts. The only network connections the program itself makes are downloading public files from bungie.net and, if you leave it on, asking GitHub whether a newer version of DeskGhost exists. The Website and Discord buttons simply hand that address to your normal browser — nothing about you goes with it.

Updates

New versions are published on GitHub. DeskGhost can look for one when it starts, and if there is one it shows an "Update available" badge and an offer in Settings.

  • Nothing installs on its own. A check only tells you. The new version is downloaded and installed only when you press Download and install yourself, and you can ignore or dismiss the offer for as long as you like.
  • You can turn checking off. Settings → "Look for updates when DeskGhost starts". With it off, DeskGhost never contacts the update server unless you press "Check now".
  • What a check sends. A plain request for one small file listing the latest version. It carries no account, no name and no identifier — nothing that says who you are. As with any web request, the server it goes to (GitHub) can see the IP address it came from. Nothing is stored by us.
  • Every update is signed. Each release is signed with a private key held by the author, and your copy verifies that signature before installing anything. A download that has been tampered with, or that came from anywhere else, is refused.
  • Your settings and downloaded shells are kept across an update — you won't have to set it up or re-download anything.

Games and anti-cheat

Anti-cheat systems act against software that runs inside a game: injected code, hooks, memory reading, automated input. DeskGhost does none of those things — it is an ordinary window, like a chat window or a notification, and it gives you no information or advantage in any game.

  • Destiny 2: Bungie's published policy is that it will not ban or restrict players for using common third-party applications such as Discord, OBS or RTSS, unless they are used to break Bungie's Code of Conduct. BattlEye, Destiny 2's anti-cheat, states it bans for actual cheats and hacks, and that non-cheat overlays are generally supported.
  • Other games: the same distinction applies to Easy Anti-Cheat, Riot Vanguard and the rest — what they block is software that reads or modifies the game.
  • It steps aside for fullscreen games. While a fullscreen app or game is in front on the Ghost's monitor, the whole overlay is hidden and stops rendering. This is on by default (Settings → "Hide during fullscreen games and videos"), and it also avoids the frame-rate and input-lag cost of any window sitting on top of a fullscreen game.
Playing in a window? The Ghost can wander across your game. Auto-hide only triggers for apps that cover the whole monitor. If you play windowed or windowed-borderless at less than full screen, the Ghost can drift over the game window, which may be distracting during play. Turn the Ghost off from the Ghost tab or the tray icon before you play that way, or move it to another monitor in Settings.

No game company guarantees anything about third-party programs, and policies change. If you play in tournaments or organised competition, follow that event's rules about what may run on your PC.
